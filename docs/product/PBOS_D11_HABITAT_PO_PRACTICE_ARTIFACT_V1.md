# PBOS D11 · Habitat PO Practice Artifact v1

**Status:** Draft for user validation  
**Gate:** DG-03  
**Purpose:** Transform an existing Habitat experience into a Product Owner practice artifact without rewriting history.

> **Important distinction:** this is a **reconstructed PO artifact created by PBOS**. It is not presented as an original document formally used by Habitat at the time. Historical facts are separated from PO-format reconstruction.

---

## 1. Historical evidence used

The artifact is grounded in the documented Habitat experience:

- Support initially occurred through WhatsApp Business, without a ticketing system and without a structured knowledge base.
- Existing explanations were generic and assumed prior knowledge of the system.
- The user created materials from scratch, including step-by-step instructions and videos.
- Habitat had different user journeys and products: the public site was used by doctors and room owners; the SaaS was used by clinic owners.
- Each material was adapted to the persona and journey instead of being generic.
- The user reordered the predefined topic list according to client need, recurrence, impact and difficulty.
- Larger needs were decomposed into smaller layers and executable steps, including website registration and SaaS material.
- The materials were validated by the owner and refined based on persistent doubts, recurrence and usage.
- Content was used for self-service, chatbot knowledge and training.
- The documented case reports more than 100 materials, 27% fewer recurring demands and 31% lower average resolution time. These results are treated as observed case outcomes and are not presented as proof of sole causality.

---

## 2. Product problem

### Problem statement

Users depended on direct support for recurring questions because available explanations were incomplete, generic or assumed prior knowledge.

This generated avoidable support contacts and reduced the ability of users to complete common journeys independently.

### Product/experience opportunity

Convert recurring user problems into clear, persona-specific knowledge that allows self-service while freeing support capacity for more complex cases.

---

## 3. Persona

### Selected practice persona: Clinic owner

The clinic owner represents the SaaS context documented in the experience.

Clinic owners with multiple consulting rooms had a different operational need from doctors and room owners. Habitat offered the SaaS specifically for this segment.

**Important:** doctors and room owners used the Habitat site and did not use the SaaS. The SaaS was not commercially offered indiscriminately because the company's return did not justify doing so.

---

## 4. User need

> As a clinic owner, I need clear and executable guidance to understand and complete relevant SaaS tasks without depending unnecessarily on direct support.

**Classification:** reconstructed User Story for PO practice.  
**Historical status:** the experience demonstrates user-focused, executable material production, but this exact User Story format was not documented as an original Habitat artifact.

---

## 5. Epic

### Epic: Enable self-service execution of recurring SaaS journeys

**Objective:** reduce unnecessary support dependency by transforming recurring user needs into understandable, executable and validated knowledge.

**Historical basis:** the user produced step-by-step materials for the Habitat website and SaaS and decomposed larger needs into several layers.

---

## 6. Epic decomposition

The following decomposition is a PO-format reconstruction based on the work actually described:

### Layer 1 · Understand the journey

- Identify what the user is trying to accomplish.
- Identify recurring doubts and friction.
- Understand the persona's context.

### Layer 2 · Define the task

- Identify the starting point.
- Identify information or access required.
- Break the larger task into executable steps.

### Layer 3 · Build the guidance

- Write clear step-by-step instructions.
- Remove assumptions about prior system knowledge.
- Adapt language and sequence to the persona.
- Produce video when useful.

### Layer 4 · Validate

- Submit material for owner validation.
- Observe persistent doubts.
- Monitor recurrence and usage.
- Identify content requiring correction or complement.

### Layer 5 · Scale

- Deliver material to users.
- Reuse written content in chatbot knowledge.
- Use material in training.

---

## 7. Prioritization logic

The historical prioritization model was not simply chronological.

The user had a predefined list of topics but exercised autonomy to reorder production according to:

1. **Client/user need**
2. **Recurrence**
3. **Impact**
4. **Difficulty**
5. **Potential value of resolving the problem through knowledge/self-service**

This demonstrates practical prioritization by user value, although it should not be described as a formal Product Backlog prioritization ceremony unless additional evidence is found.

## 9. Reconstructed Acceptance Criteria

The following criteria are deliberately labeled as reconstruction. They express what would constitute a successful PO deliverable based on the historical work.

### For the knowledge material

- A user without prior knowledge of the system can follow the instructions.
- The journey is divided into clear executable steps.
- Required information/access is identified before execution.
- The content is appropriate to the selected persona and product context.
- The owner can validate whether the material represents the expected process.
- Persistent doubts can be identified after publication.
- The material can be reused in self-service, chatbot knowledge or training when applicable.

These are **practice acceptance criteria**, not claims that Habitat formally recorded them in this exact form.

## 10. Refinement logic

The historical work demonstrates refinement through progressive decomposition:

**Large need**  
→ understand the user problem  
→ identify the relevant persona  
→ break the journey into layers  
→ define executable steps  
→ produce material  
→ validate  
→ identify remaining doubts  
→ improve the material.

This supports the competency **Backlog Refinement / requirement refinement** at a practical level, without claiming that the user participated in a formal Scrum refinement ceremony.

## 11. Validation loop

The experience supports a continuous validation cycle:

**User problem**  
↓  
**Material produced**  
↓  
**Owner validation**  
↓  
**User usage**  
↓  
**Persistent doubts / recurrence**  
↓  
**Correction or complement**  
↓  
**Reuse at scale**

The key Product Owner capability demonstrated here is not merely producing documentation. It is using observed user problems to improve the experience and reduce operational friction.

## 14. Recruiter-facing interpretation

This artifact should not be presented as evidence that the user formally held a Product Owner title at Habitat.

Its value is different:

> It demonstrates that the user can take a real customer problem, understand the affected persona, decompose the problem, prioritize by value, structure executable requirements, define validation conditions and close the loop through observed user behavior and outcomes.

That is the bridge between the user's historical experience and the language expected in Product Owner hiring processes.

## 15. Interview narrative

A concise interview version can follow this logic:

> “Na Habitat, eu percebi que uma parte relevante dos contatos de suporte vinha de dúvidas recorrentes. Existia uma lista inicial de temas, mas eu não tratava aquilo simplesmente como uma fila. Eu reorganizava a produção considerando necessidade do cliente, recorrência, impacto e dificuldade. Quando o problema era maior, eu quebrava em camadas menores e transformava a necessidade em passos que alguém sem conhecimento prévio pudesse executar. Isso aconteceu tanto nos materiais do site quanto nos materiais relacionados ao SaaS, sempre considerando a persona e a jornada. Depois, o material era validado, acompanhado pelo comportamento dos usuários e ajustado conforme as dúvidas persistentes e a recorrência.”
