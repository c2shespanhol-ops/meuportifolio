## Governance decision

D15 is approved as a **reconstructed Product Analytics and Product Metrics practice artifact**.

It develops the ability to reason from product questions to outcomes, metrics, signals, segmentation, interpretation, causality and decisions. It does not establish historical ownership of Product Analytics, formal Product KPIs, analytics instrumentation, experimentation, North Star Metrics or executive analytics decisions.

Historical evidence and reconstructed practice remain explicitly separated.

## Validated scope

1. D15 develops practical Product Analytics and Product Metrics reasoning: selecting useful measures, connecting metrics to product questions and outcomes, interpreting signals, identifying limitations and translating evidence into decisions.

2. D15 is a reconstructed practice artifact and must not be presented as historical formal ownership of Product Analytics, Product KPIs or an analytics function.

3. The next competency step is the connection: **Problem → User → Outcome → Metric → Signal → Interpretation → Decision → Learning**, using already validated Habitat signals such as recurring demands, resolution time, material usage, persistent doubts and user behavior.

4. Habitat is the primary practice case because it contains multiple observable user, support and operational signals, including observed reductions in recurring demands and average resolution time, without establishing formal Product Analytics ownership.

5. The metric-selection framework uses five filters: product question, user behavior, signal, desired outcome and resulting decision.

6. Metrics are separated into outcome metrics, behavioral/product signals and operational metrics. An operational improvement is not automatically a product outcome improvement.

7. The reconstructed metric tree centers on increasing user autonomy in recurring SaaS journeys while reducing avoidable support dependency, with outcome, diagnostic and operational indicators beneath it.

8. Metric interpretation requires asking what changed, where, for whom and what plausible explanation should be investigated. A metric movement does not prove causality.

9. Segmentation must respect Habitat's product/persona structure: doctors and room owners use the site, while clinic owners use the SaaS. SaaS metrics should focus on clinic-owner journeys rather than mixing populations that do not use the same product.

10. Metrics must lead to investigation and decisions. Examples include investigating persistent friction when recurring demand remains high, distinguishing access from successful task completion, avoiding declaring product success from resolution time alone, and adapting experiences when personas show materially different friction.

11. Competencies exercised include Product Metrics, Product Analytics reasoning, Outcome Thinking, metric selection and hierarchy, Metric Trees, segmentation, data interpretation, causality discipline, evidence-based decision making, Product Thinking and connection between metrics and backlog decisions.

12. D15 does not establish historical ownership of Product Analytics, formal Product KPIs, analytics stack, event instrumentation, statistical experimentation, North Star Metric ownership or executive product decisions based solely on analytics.
