# PBOS D16 · Agile & Product Language Practice · v1

**Status:** Draft for user validation  
**Gate:** DG-03 — Product Owner Positioning & Development Plan  
**Priority:** P2  
**Base evidence:** D11 Habitat + D12 PAV + D13 Product Backlog Mechanics + D14 Product Management Practice + D15 Product Analytics & Metrics Practice

## 1. Purpose

Strengthen the user's fluency in the language commonly used in Product Owner and Product Management environments, especially the ability to describe decisions, artifacts and ways of working using market-recognizable terminology without converting reconstructed practice into historical claims.

D16 is a **practice artifact**. It is not intended to claim that the user historically performed formal Scrum ceremonies, held a formal Product Owner title or used every listed artifact in a formal framework.

## 2. Why this is the next step

DG-03 identified formal Agile/Product language as a P2 development priority after the P0 Evidence Conversion and Product Backlog Mechanics work and the P1 Product Management and Product Analytics work.

The objective is not to restart basic Agile learning. The user already has strong Kanban knowledge and certifications. The focus is translation: connecting what was actually done to the vocabulary recruiters and product teams recognize.

## 3. Core translation principle

D16 uses the rule:

**Historical action → accurate Product terminology → evidence boundary.**

The terminology should make the experience easier to understand, not make the experience sound more senior than the evidence supports.

## 4. Translation map

### Problem Discovery

Historical experience: recurring user doubts, support conversations, behavior and friction were observed to identify problems.

Market language: **Discovery, Problem Discovery, User Research inputs, Customer Insights**.

Boundary: do not imply formal UX Research ownership where it is not documented.

### Persona and Journey

Historical experience: materials and experiences were adapted to doctors, room owners and clinic owners according to their different product contexts and journeys.

Market language: **Personas, User Journey, Customer Journey, User-Centered Product Thinking**.

Boundary: do not imply ownership of a formal Design Research methodology without evidence.

### Requirements

Historical experience: needs were translated into concrete implementation requirements, including physical/technical readiness in the PAV and executable guidance in Habitat.

Market language: **Requirements Gathering, Requirements Definition, Functional Requirements, Requirements Decomposition**.

Boundary: do not imply sole ownership when the work was collaborative.

### Decomposition

Historical experience: larger needs were broken into layers and smaller executable/validatable steps, including website registration and SaaS guidance.

Market language: **Decomposition, Epic Breakdown, User Story decomposition**.

Boundary: User Story terminology is reconstructed unless an original formal story artifact exists.

### Prioritization

Historical experience: Habitat topics were reordered according to client/user need, recurrence, impact, difficulty and value/autonomy potential.

Market language: **Value-based Prioritization, Backlog Prioritization, Opportunity Prioritization**.

Boundary: do not claim formal Scrum Product Backlog prioritization where the historical process was not formally documented as Scrum.

### Validation

Historical experience: owner validation, user validation, observation of persistent doubts and usage, and readiness checks were part of the work.

Market language: **Validation, User Validation, Stakeholder Validation, Acceptance Thinking**.

Boundary: do not claim formal Acceptance Criteria as historical artifacts unless original evidence exists.

### Continuous Improvement

Historical experience: doubts, recurrence, behavior and operational signals were used to identify corrections and improvements.

Market language: **Continuous Improvement, Feedback Loop, Iterative Improvement, Inspect and Adapt**.

Boundary: do not attribute the work to a formal Scrum ceremony unless documented.

## 5. Agile framework language

D16 distinguishes concepts that are useful in interviews from formal historical claims.

### Safe to use as conceptual language

- Agile mindset;
- iterative improvement;
- incremental delivery;
- prioritization by value;
- feedback loop;
- customer/user focus;
- backlog thinking;
- refinement thinking;
- cross-functional collaboration;
- transparency of work;
- continuous improvement;
- flow and Lead Time;
- Kanban.

### Use with explicit reconstruction or qualification

- User Story;
- Acceptance Criteria;
- Product Backlog Refinement;
- Sprint Planning;
- Daily Scrum;
- Sprint Review;
- Sprint Retrospective;
- Definition of Done;
- formal Scrum Product Owner responsibilities.

The user's Kanban knowledge should be treated as a strong existing competency, supported by TKP, KMP, KSD and KSI, rather than as a basic learning gap.

## 6. Product language translation

D16 reinforces market language already developed in D11–D15:

**Discovery → Problem → Persona → Journey → Requirement → Decomposition → Prioritization → Backlog → Validation → Metric → Decision → Learning.**

This sequence connects the user's practical evidence to a recognizable Product operating model.

## 7. Interview application

When describing an experience, prefer:

1. What was the problem?
2. Who was affected?
3. What did I actually do?
4. How did I prioritize?
5. What did I validate?
6. What signal/result did I observe?
7. What changed or what did I learn?

Then select the Product terminology that accurately describes the action.

Example:

Instead of: “Eu fazia documentação para o suporte.”

Prefer: “Eu identifiquei problemas recorrentes na jornada do usuário, priorizei os temas pelo valor e recorrência, decompondo necessidades em orientações executáveis e validando o material antes de escalar o conhecimento para autoatendimento, chatbot e treinamento.”

This describes the actual experience while making its Product relevance explicit.

- User Journey;
- Requirements Gathering;
- Requirements Decomposition;
- Prioritization;
- Product Backlog thinking;
- Validation;
- Continuous Improvement;
- Product Thinking;
- Agile mindset;
- Kanban and Flow;

- evidence-based decision making.

## 9. Explicit limitations

D16 does not establish historical evidence of:

- formal Scrum ceremony ownership;
- formal Scrum Product Owner title;
- formal Sprint ownership;
- formal Definition of Done ownership;
- formal Product Backlog ownership in a Scrum environment;
- formal Agile transformation leadership;
- E2E Product Ownership.

## 10. Expected outcome

The intended outcome is not simply knowing more terminology. It is being able to communicate existing experience in the language of Product without exaggeration, ambiguity or loss of evidence integrity.

## 11. Next validation gate

Validation will occur incrementally, two points at a time. The user should validate whether the translation accurately represents the already validated experiences and whether any terminology risks overstating historical responsibility.
