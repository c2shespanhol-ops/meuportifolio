## Validated scope

The user confirmed the reconstructed mechanics covering:

1. Selection of Habitat as the primary practice case because it concentrates recurring user problems, personas, SaaS context, decomposition, prioritization, validation and improvement.
2. The underlying problem of recurring user dependence on direct support due to generic/incomplete information and assumed prior knowledge.
3. The reconstructed Product Backlog hierarchy: Epic → capability areas → backlog items → User Story → Acceptance Criteria.
4. The four capability areas: understand recurring user problems; structure guidance for priority journeys; validate guidance with users/owner; scale validated knowledge across channels.
5. The reconstructed backlog items and their P0/P1/P2 prioritization.
6. The reconstructed User Story for the clinic-owner SaaS persona.
7. The reconstructed Acceptance Criteria for clear, executable, persona-appropriate guidance.
8. The reconstructed refinement sequence from recurring problem through decomposition, validation, improvement and scale.
9. The dependency logic among persona, journey, decomposition, validation, behavior/doubts and scaling.
10. The trade-offs represented by the experience: speed vs completeness; coverage vs value; generic vs persona-specific guidance; manual support vs scalable knowledge.
11. The Product Owner competencies exercised by the artifact: Epic structuring, backlog decomposition, User Story reconstruction, Acceptance Criteria, refinement thinking, prioritization, dependencies, trade-offs, user value, validation, continuous improvement and Product Thinking.
12. The explicit limitations: no historical evidence established by D13 for formal Scrum refinement ceremonies, formal PO title, Product Vision ownership, Product Strategy ownership, roadmap ownership, formal Product Analytics ownership or E2E Product Ownership.

## Claim boundary

The artifact demonstrates the ability to translate validated experience into recognizable Product Owner mechanics. It does not change the historical nature of the Habitat experience or create evidence that did not exist.
