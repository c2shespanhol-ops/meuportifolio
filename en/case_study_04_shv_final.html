CASE 04 · SHV ENERGY

Customer onboarding and monitoring for LPG supply

An operational experience centered on the customer's first deliveries, combining guidance, preventive monitoring, logistics escalation, Salesforce validation and the history needed to transition to automatic supply.

Context
Large-scale operation
System
Salesforce
Safety margin
35%
Emergency window
Up to 48 business hours

The operational problem

The risk did not start when the customer ran out of gas. Weekly monitoring existed to identify the approach of the safety margin in advance because emergency supply could take up to 48 business hours and depended on operational availability.

Reconstructed journey

01 · ONBOARDING

Orientation and initial expectations

Company presentation, service guidance, consumption education and explanation of the safety margin. A three-question satisfaction survey was also used to assess technical capability and organization of teams involved in construction, installation and supply.

02 · WEEKLY MONITORING

Preventive follow-up

During the first deliveries, customers were contacted weekly to report tank levels. The 35% reference acted as a preventive margin.

03 · EMERGENCY ACTION

Logistics escalation

When necessary, an emergency refill was requested from logistics. Customers were also instructed to contact the service center if they reached the safety margin before the next scheduled contact.

04 · VALIDATION

Salesforce plus customer feedback

Salesforce records showed whether the refill had been registered. The following weekly contact also confirmed the event directly with the customer.

05 · CONSUMPTION HISTORY

Building the operating pattern

The first five deliveries formed the history needed to calculate average consumption and support the definition of refill intervals.

06 · HANDOFF

Transition to automatic supply

After this stage, the customer moved to automatic supply and future needs were directed to the service center.

Process limitations

Some customers still ran out of gas because guidance was ignored or teams were unavailable on the desired date.

What this case demonstrates

The experience does not represent formal Product Management ownership. It demonstrates, within the real scope of the role, Customer Onboarding, Customer Journey, preventive monitoring, operational risk management, Customer Validation, CRM/Salesforce, operational data, cross-functional coordination and handoff. These capabilities are transferable to Product Owner roles while remaining grounded in the actual operational experience.
