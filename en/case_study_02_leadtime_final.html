CASE STUDY 02 · PRODUCT GOVERNANCE

Product Governance for Operational Lead Time Reduction

How I organized an uncontrolled service-request flow, increased user visibility and used process data to reduce delays and rework.

DOCUMENTED RESULT
71% Lead Time reduction · requests resolved in up to 48 hours
Client

Secretariat of Tax and Fiscal Management (SEGET)

Context

Municipal Planning and Modernization

Role

Product Operations | UX

Focus

Flow, traceability and user experience

01 · THE PROBLEM

The problem was not only delay.

Requests entered without control

Users and stakeholders frequently reached out for help. Many requests had no protocol, deadline, owner or clear way to track progress. Some requests were not completed at all.

The user lost control

The challenge became twofold: establish control over requests and identify where the process was losing time.

02 · HOW I ACTED

First I organized the flow. Then I looked for speed.

01 / UNDERSTAND

Listen to users and stakeholders

I observed recurring problems and conversations from day-to-day service.

02 / RESEARCH

Select the right tool

I already knew Trello, but needed a more dynamic way to manage tickets. I researched alternatives and selected Jira.

03 / ORGANIZE

Build the Kanban flow

Responsible owners, categories, priorities, status and deadlines became visible.

04 / PRIORITIZE

Prioritize by impact and value

Requests were not handled simply by arrival order. Impact and user value guided prioritization.

05 / MONITOR

Use service commitments

The IT Service Charter became the deadline reference, with an internal 24-hour buffer for follow-up.

06 / DECIDE

Escalate with evidence

When the official deadline was missed, I had evidence to challenge the treatment and request reclassification as an incident when applicable.

07 / IMPROVE

Look for causes

I analyzed bottlenecks and causes of delay instead of simply asking for more speed.

08 / SIMPLIFY

Reduce interpretation

For system-access requests, screenshots clarified exactly what the user needed and reduced rework.

09 / VALIDATE

Close only after user validation

A ticket was not considered complete just because IT responded. The user tested the solution and confirmed it worked.

03 · WHAT CHANGED

When the flow became visible, the bottlenecks became visible too.

Access requests

Clearer requests reduced interpretation and rework.

Technical visits

Exceptions became trackable and could be challenged with evidence when deadlines were exceeded.

User validation

The response stopped being the end of the process. The user confirmed the solution worked.

Management perception

Managers noticed that requests that once took a week, and sometimes up to a month, were being resolved faster.

04 · RESULT & LEARNING

The number shows the result. The process explains why.

71% Lead Time reduction, with requests reaching resolution in up to 48 hours.

The work combined Jira charts with conversations during delivery. The main learning was simple: before trying to accelerate a process, understand where the user loses control, where information arrives incomplete and where the flow starts to stop.
