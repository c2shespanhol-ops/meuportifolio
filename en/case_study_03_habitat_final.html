CASE 03 · HABITAT CONSULTÓRIOS INTELIGENTES

From individual support to self-service.

A multifunctional experience that started in CX and user support and also involved Product and Scrum practices, knowledge management, SaaS operations, chatbot support, commercial journey work and content for different personas.

Company
Habitat Consultórios Inteligentes
Scope
CX · Product · Scrum · SaaS · Discovery · UX · Operations · Commercial
Tools
WhatsApp Business · ClickUp · Pipedrive
Result
27% fewer recurring requests

The problem

Unstructured support

Support happened through WhatsApp. There was no ticketing system and no structured knowledge base.

Knowledge was hard to use

Generic explanations assumed prior product knowledge and did not always help users complete a journey.

Support dependency

Recurring questions consumed operational time even when users could solve them with the right guidance.

Misaligned expectations

Users contacted the company outside business hours without knowing when they would receive an answer.

What I did

01 · DISCOVERY

Observed real user behavior

I used support conversations, recurring questions, user behavior and real cases to identify friction and unmet needs.

02 · PRIORITIZATION

Prioritized by user value

I had autonomy to change the order of a predefined topic list based on customer need, recurrence, impact and difficulty observed in support.

03 · REQUIREMENTS

Decomposed larger journeys

When a need involved a larger journey, I broke it into smaller executable steps and content layers.

04 · UX

Built persona-oriented guidance

I transformed generic explanations into step-by-step materials aligned with each persona and journey, using videos when useful.

05 · VALIDATION

Validated in practice

I observed remaining doubts and recurring requests to identify what needed correction or additional explanation.

06 · SCALE

Turned knowledge into self-service

Videos were sent directly to users. Written content supported the chatbot and internal training.

One solution served three needs: helping the user, supporting the chatbot and enabling internal training.

Product and Scrum in practice

Product Backlog

I worked with the demand and feature list and helped structure the work. Final prioritization remained with the product owner.

Backlog Refinement

I helped clarify and detail work before development, reducing ambiguity about customer needs.

Sprint Planning

I participated in defining the work and translating customer needs into development context.

Daily Scrum

I participated and, when facilitating, organized follow-up, identified blockers and helped route impediments.

Sprint Review

I checked whether delivered functionality addressed the need that originated the work.

Sprint Retrospective

I participated in identifying process problems and improvements for the next cycle.

Core contribution: I brought the real customer problem into the development conversation and had the autonomy to challenge a delivery when it did not solve the underlying need.

This case describes practices actually performed. It does not imply sole responsibility for backlog ownership, final prioritization, product strategy or all formal Product Owner/Scrum Master responsibilities.

From perception to evidence

Customer Insights

Real cases, recurring questions and observed behavior shaped problem understanding.

Evidence-based decisions

I brought concrete user evidence into product discussions when my perception differed from the initial stakeholder view.

Value-based prioritization

Recurrence, impact, user need and autonomy potential helped distinguish meaningful problems from noise.

Stakeholder influence

I influenced decisions by putting customer evidence into the conversation without replacing the final decision maker.

Important distinction: this reasoning resembles the principle behind approaches such as the Kano Model, but I did not formally use Kano at Habitat and do not present it as a methodology I applied there.
