CASE 02 · DOCUMENT 03 · SERVICE DESIGN

Service Workflow Architecture

English version of the original supporting document. The content preserves the scope, reported outcomes and evidence limitations of the source.

01 · Workflow

From need to resolution

  1. 01 · IntakeEmployee submits the need through the ticket portal.
  2. 02 · CurationPlanning and Modernization reviews the request, documentation and context.
  3. 03 · Jira registrationThe ticket receives structured tracking.
  4. 04 · ClassificationIncident or service request, according to the service and context.
  5. 05 · ExecutionTIC performs the technical activity.
  6. 06 · MonitoringPlanning monitors deadlines and inactivity.
  7. 07 · CommunicationThe response is relayed to the employee.
  8. 08 · ValidationThe employee tests the access or solution with support.
  9. 09 · ClosureThe ticket closes after effective validation.
02 · Quality gates

Controls in the workflow

  • Complete form and appropriate documentation.
  • Classification consistent with the Service Charter.
  • Active deadline monitoring.
  • Follow-up when a ticket becomes inactive.
  • Solution validation by the employee.
  • Prompt correction when delivered access differs from the request.
03 · Experience design

The employee at the center

The process was designed so employees did not need to understand TIC's internal structure. When there was uncertainty about ticket type, documentation or form completion, guidance was provided directly. The goal was to reduce intake friction and improve the quality of the information received.

04 · Outcome

Resolution-oriented workflow

The workflow stopped treating a technical response as the finish line. The journey ended only when the employee's need had actually been met.
