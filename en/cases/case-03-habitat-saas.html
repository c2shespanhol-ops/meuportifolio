Case 03 · Habitat Consultórios Inteligentes

From individual support to self-service

How WhatsApp-based support without tickets or a knowledge base became persona-based content for users, the chatbot and training.

About 5 min read

Reported results
27% fewer recurring demands and 31% lower average resolution time
Deliverables
More than 100 materials created
Role
Digital Product Analyst | Customer Experience
Company and period
Habitat Consultórios Inteligentes (B2B SaaS), October 2023 to January 2025
Tools
WhatsApp Business, ClickUp and Pipedrive

My role: Discovery, backlog organization and detailing, delivery follow-up, facilitation of Scrum events, functional testing and validation, lead management and content production. Evidence boundary: the company owner made the final decisions on product direction and feature priorities. This case does not claim formal ownership of the Product Backlog or Product Strategy.

01 · The problem

Support without structure

No ticketing system or knowledge base

Support took place through WhatsApp, without a ticketing system or structured knowledge.

Content that assumed prior knowledge

The existing generic explanations assumed users already knew how to use the platform.

Dependence on support

Recurring questions consumed operational time and could have been resolved by users themselves.

Misaligned expectations

Messages received outside business hours had no indication of when a response could be expected.

02 · Personas

Three profiles, three different needs

Physicians

Used the website to find and use consultation rooms. They did not use the SaaS.

Room owners

Also used the website, following a different journey from physicians. The SaaS was not intended for them.

Clinic owners

With multiple rooms, they needed to manage operations. Habitat offered the SaaS to this profile.

Business logic

The SaaS was offered where user needs and commercial returns justified the product, rather than indiscriminately.

This distinction guided knowledge production: each material was designed for the relevant persona and journey.

03 · What I did

From observed problems to self-service

  1. DiscoveryI used support conversations, recurring questions and real cases to identify usability problems and friction points.
  2. PriorizaçãoThere was a predefined list of topics, but I could change the order based on customer needs, recurrence, impact and observed difficulty.
  3. Requirements and decompositionLarger journeys were broken down into layers and smaller steps, as in the website registration material and SaaS documentation.
  4. UX and content developmentEach piece of content was based on the persona's journey and enabled someone without prior knowledge to complete the task through step-by-step instructions and, when applicable, video.
  5. ValidationIn addition to the owner's review, I observed usage, remaining questions and recurring requests to correct or supplement the content.
  6. EscalaVideos were sent directly to users. Written content fed the chatbot and training. One solution, three uses.
04 · Product and evidence

Bring the real customer into decision-making

I participated in refinement, planning, daily stand-ups, reviews and retrospectives, and facilitated the daily when it was my turn. The key was bringing real customer needs into discussions with Development and returning deliveries for adjustment when they did not solve the problem.

Evidence over perception

When my understanding of the customer differed from the owner's, I brought concrete examples and, when available, connected them to the business metrics being monitored. In some cases, this changed the perspective on what should be considered.

Influence without being the decision-maker

My role was not to replace the owner. It was to bring customer evidence into the discussion so decisions considered the problems observed at the point of use.

05 · From need to delivery

User stories, acceptance criteria and testing

  1. Reconstructed user storyAs a physician, I want to understand each product's features, rules and conditions of use so I can identify which one fits my professional stage and purchase and use it to book a room.
  2. Acceptance criteriaProduct name, purpose, career stage, price, compatible rooms, usage rules, cancellation period, how to purchase, check the balance and use the product to book a room.
  3. UAT, OAT and functional testingI participated in business and technology validation, observing user behavior and completion of the expected action. The available records do not substantiate controlled A/B experiments.
06 · Operations and conversion

Customer experience and commercial journey

Response-time expectations

I configured a WhatsApp Business away message showing service hours.

Continuous improvement

When content did not resolve a question, the correction went back to Operations. After IT or Development changes, I asked the user to test before closing the request.

Leads

I managed interested users as leads in Pipedrive, from first contact through follow-up and negotiation. The initial order followed arrival time and was later adjusted according to urgency, interest and stage.

Conversion bottlenecks

When the owner questioned bottlenecks, I brought real cases and connected operational observations to the conversion metrics he monitored.
