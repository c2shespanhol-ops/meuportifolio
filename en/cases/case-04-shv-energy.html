CASE 04 · SHV ENERGY · OPERATIONS · CONTINUOUS IMPROVEMENT

Efficiency starts at the front line of operations.

How direct visibility into user behavior, friction identification and continuous improvement of customer-service flows contributed to lower response time and higher productivity in a national operation.

REPORTED RESULTS40% less response time · 20% higher operational productivity
COMPANYSHV Energy
PERIODJanuary 2018 · June 2023
CONTRIBUTIONOperations · Continuous Improvement · User Experience
CONTEXTNational customer-service operation
ROLE & SCOPE

Operational experience as a source of user insight.

My contribution: direct work in operations and customer service, identifying friction, organizing flows, structuring onboarding, connecting customer, operations and logistics, and participating in functional tests before new functionality was released. Limit: this case demonstrates continuous-improvement and operational-UX practice; it does not claim formal ownership of Product Strategy or Product Backlog.

01 · CONTEXT & PROBLEM

A scaled operation with friction costing time and experience.

Response time

Critical requests required better flow organization and communication to reduce response time.

Unstructured onboarding

The initial customer journey needed clearer follow-up, including the first supply cycles.

Front-line friction

Direct customer-service work made it possible to identify recurring difficulties, system issues and process impacts.

Operational integration

Communication between customer, service, operations and logistics had to remain aligned throughout onboarding and support.

02 · IMPROVEMENT PROCESS

Five years of operations turned into continuous learning.

01 · Friction identification

Observed real user behavior at the service front line and identified recurring patterns of difficulty and bottlenecks.

02 · Customer onboarding

Structured the initial journey, including first contact, consumption definition and follow-up of the first supply cycles.

03 · Communication flows

Improved communication between customer, operations and logistics to reduce response time and increase efficiency.

04 · Functional validation

Participated in functional testing before release to operations, bringing the service perspective into validation.

03 · MEASURABLE RESULTS

Improvements built in the real operation.

40%

Response time

Reported reduction in critical requests after improvements to flow and communication.

20%

Productivity

Reported increase in operational productivity through continuous improvement of routines and processes.

5+

Years of operation

Accumulated experience in a national-scale operation with direct observation of user behavior.

04 · IMPACT & PRODUCT VIEW

Operations as a source of product insight.

Operational UX

Customer-service experience made it possible to identify where users struggled, what caused confusion and which processes created friction.

Onboarding

The initial journey was treated as an important part of the experience, with follow-up of early steps and operational integration.

Stakeholders

Acted as a link between customer, operations and logistics to keep the journey and support aligned.

UAT and validation

Participated in functional tests with an operational perspective, helping identify inconsistencies before release.

05 · LEARNINGS

What this experience reinforced professionally.

UX starts at the operational front line

The practice of identifying friction and understanding users emerged before UX was formalized as a discipline in my work.

Continuous improvement happens in cycles

Successive adjustments to flows, communication and information organization can create cumulative gains without depending on a formal project.

Onboarding shapes the journey

Structuring the customer's first steps helps make the experience clearer and more predictable.

Operational validation brings users closer to product

Bringing the customer-service perspective into functional tests increases the ability to identify issues before release.

Evidence boundary: the results and descriptions in this case reflect the documented experience. The case does not turn operational work into a claim of formal responsibility for product strategy or backlog.
