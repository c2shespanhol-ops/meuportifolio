CASE 05 · FEDERAL REVENUE PAV · MARICÁ

Bringing the service to citizens without creating a new path from scratch.

Participation in Discovery, requirements structuring, alignment with the Federal Revenue Service and implementation of Virtual Service Points in three regions of Maricá.

ContextMaricá City Hall · SEGET
PracticeDiscovery · Service Design · Product Ops
StakeholdersCitizens · City Hall · Federal Revenue Service
ScaleImplementation in 3 units
My role: I participated in Discovery, journey mapping, requirements definition, alignment with the Federal Revenue Service, benchmarking, implementation and validation. Limite: the PAV model and its service structure already existed at the Federal Revenue Service; my contribution was local, not independent creation of the service.

The problem

Distant access

To resolve certain issues, Maricá citizens had to go to Federal Revenue Service units outside the municipality, making in-person service more difficult and costly.

A local opportunity

The City Hall's demand created an opportunity to bring some of these services closer to residents through the Federal Revenue Service's Virtual Service Point model.

It was not about creating a service from scratch

The Federal Revenue Service already had a PAV model used in other municipalities and an established service structure. The challenge was to understand the local problem and enable implementation within that model.

Inter-organizational dependency

The solution required alignment between City Hall and the Federal Revenue Service, as well as physical, technological, operational and service requirements.

Discovery

01 · UNDERSTAND THE PROBLEM

I participated in conversations with citizens

I contributed to understanding the need to bring service closer and the difficulties faced by people needing access to Federal Revenue Service services.

02 · MAP THE JOURNEY

I participated in journey mapping

The journey was analyzed to understand how citizens reached the need, which steps were involved and where local service could facilitate access.

03 · ALIGN

I interacted directly with the Federal Revenue Service

I participated in discussions with the Federal Revenue Service to understand the existing model, implementation conditions and how the operation could work in Maricá.

04 · DEFINE REQUIREMENTS

I participated in requirements definition

I helped translate identified needs into implementation requirements, considering physical structure, equipment, system access, training and operations.

Limite importante: I did not define or prioritize the Service Charter and did not define the solution alone. The model and service structure were already established by the Federal Revenue Service.

From benchmark to implementation

A reference point

I visited an existing PAV unit to observe, in practice, equipment, layout, space organization, service types, service execution, support, system access and training.

Local adaptation

Lessons from the visit were used as a reference for implementation in Maricá, respecting the Federal Revenue Service model and local conditions.

Three units

Implementation took place at the Municipal Integrated Services units in Centro, Itaipuaçu and São José.

Follow-up

I participated in delivery follow-up, user validation and subsequent support when operational needs arose.

How the service works

In-person service

The PAV is a partner unit, not a Federal Revenue Service agency. In-person service is provided by the partner entity's team.

Triage and guidance

The team assesses the request, guides the citizen and checks whether digital channels can be used.

Digital routing

When necessary, documents and requests are routed digitally to the Federal Revenue Service teams responsible for analysis.

Support

The model includes support channels, periodic meetings and technical visits to sustain operations.

Official Federal Revenue Service documentation describes the PAV as an in-person service channel operated by a partner entity, with guidance, triage and digital routing of requests.
