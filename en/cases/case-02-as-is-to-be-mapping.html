CASE 02 · DOCUMENT 02 · PROCESS DESIGN

AS IS / TO BE Workflow Mapping

English version of the original supporting document. The content preserves the scope, reported outcomes and evidence limitations of the source.

01 · AS IS

Previous workflow

Employee

Submitted requests individually and had to discover how and with whom to address each need.

Information and Communication Technology Department (TIC)

Received requests directly, without a curation and follow-up layer from Planning and Modernization.

Key issues included low traceability, fragmented communication, no active monitoring, low predictability, inconsistent classification, access requests based on profile mirroring and solutions not always validated by the employee.

02 · TO BE

Redesigned journey

  1. 01Employee identifies a need.
  2. 02Employee submits the request through the portal.
  3. 03Planning and Modernization receives and curates the request.
  4. 04The ticket is managed and classified in Jira.
  5. 05The request is routed to TIC.
  6. 06Planning monitors deadlines and ticket activity.
  7. 07TIC performs the work and reports the outcome.
  8. 08The employee tests the solution with support.
  9. 09Corrections are requested when necessary.
  10. 10The ticket is closed after validation.
03 · Design principles

Process decisions

  • Centralization: one layer coordinates the journey between employee and TIC.
  • Curation: requests are understood, classified and routed before execution.
  • Traceability: Jira supports request monitoring.
  • Validation: a technical response is not sufficient; the solution must work for the employee.
04 · Outcome

From fragmented flow to governed journey

The transformation combined centralization, standardization, monitoring and validation. The reported outcome was approximately 71% reduction in lead time, with greater predictability for employees and more institutional control over the workflow.
