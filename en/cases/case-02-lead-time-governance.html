Case 02 · Governança de fluxo

Request flow governance and lead time reduction

How I organized an uncontrolled request flow, restored user visibility and used process data to reduce delays and rework.

About 3 min read

Reported result
71% less lead time: from approximately one week to up to 48 hours
Rework
31% less rework caused by incomplete records
Tools
Jira and Kanban
Period
January 2025 to May 2026
Client
SEGET, Maricá City Hall, served by the Undersecretariat of Planning and Modernization

My role: I structured the Jira and Kanban workflow, defined prioritization, monitored deadlines, coordinated with IT and validated each delivery with the user. Evidence boundary: this case demonstrates Product practice and flow governance, not formal Product Owner ownership.

01 · The problem

The problem was not only the delay

Uncontrolled requests

Users and stakeholders requested help without a ticket, deadline, owner or way to track progress. In some cases, requests were never completed.

Users had no visibility

As I organized tracking, I also realized the flow was slow. The challenge became twofold: establish control and identify where the process was losing time.

02 · How I acted

First I organized the flow. Then I worked on speed.

  1. EntenderI spoke with users and stakeholders and observed recurring service problems.
  2. OrganizarI was familiar with Trello, but needed a more dynamic tool. I researched alternatives, selected Jira and structured a Kanban workflow with owners, categories, priorities, statuses and deadlines.
  3. PriorizarRequests were not handled in arrival order. The main criteria were impact and value for the user.
  4. AcompanharI used the IT Service Charter as the deadline reference and created an internal 24-hour monitoring buffer.
  5. DecidirWhen the official deadline was missed, I had evidence to follow up with IT and, when applicable, request that the ticket be reclassified from a service request to an incident.
  6. Analisar gargalosI looked for the causes of delays instead of simply asking for faster delivery.
  7. SimplificarFor system-access requests, I agreed with IT to use screenshots, removing ambiguity from the request.
  8. ValidarA ticket was closed only after the user tested the solution and confirmed that the issue was resolved.
03 · What changed

Once the flow became visible, bottlenecks emerged

System access

Screenshots clarified which access permissions were required and reduced cases where the access granted differed from what had been requested.

Technical visits

These requests became trackable. When the reference deadline was exceeded, there was evidence to question the handling and reclassify the request.

Users

An IT response was no longer the end of the process. The user tested the solution, and only then was the ticket closed.

Managers

Managers saw requests that had taken a week, and sometimes up to a month, being handled faster.

04 · Results and learning

The metric shows the result. The process explains why.

Before · approximately 1 week
168h
After · up to 48 hours
48h

Comparison basis: the 71% reduction compares approximately one week (168 hours) with up to 48 hours. This does not mean every request was completed within that time. Monitoring combined Jira charts with conversations during delivery.

Key learning: before trying to speed up a process, understand where users lose control, where information arrives incomplete and where the workflow starts to stall.
