CASE STUDY 02 · PRODUCT GOVERNANCE

Product Governance for Reducing Lead Time Operational

How I organized an uncontrolled request flow, gave users visibility and used the process data itself to reduce delays and rework.

REPORTED RESULT
71% Lead Time reduction · up to 48 hours
ClientTax and Fiscal Management Department (SEGET)
ContextUndersecretariat of Planning and Modernization
PracticeProduct Operations | UX
FocusFlow, traceability and user experience
ROLE & OWNERSHIP

What I did and what this case proves.

My contribution: I structured the operational flow using Jira, Kanban, prioritization, SLA monitoring, interaction with IT and user validation. Limite: the case demonstrates Product practice and flow governance, but is not presented as formal end-to-end Product Owner ownership.
02A · REQUEST LIFECYCLE

From intake to validation,
a traceable flow.

01 · INTAKE

Structured request

User requests began entering Jira with standardized information, reducing ambiguity and rework at the source.

02 · TRIAGE AND PRIORITIZATION

Decision within the flow

Requests were analyzed and prioritized according to operational impact and urgency, with Kanban tracking and service criteria.

Escopo da evidência: request flow management, standardized intake, operational prioritization, Jira/Kanban, traceability and tracking through delivery and validation. The case does not turn this experience into a claim of formal product ownership.
01 · THE PROBLEM

The problem was not just delay

WHAT I NOTICED

Requests entered without control

In daily work, users and stakeholders came to me for help. Many requests had no ticket, deadline, owner or clear way to track progress. In some cases, the request simply was not completed.

THE IMPACT

The user lost control of their own request

After understanding this problem, I also realized that the flow was slow. The challenge became twofold: create control over requests and then identify where the process was losing time.

02 · HOW I ACTED

First I organized the flow. Then I looked for speed.

01 / UNDERSTAND
I spoke with users and stakeholders and observed recurring service problems.
02 / RESEARCH
I already knew Trello, but I needed something more dynamic to control tickets. I researched alternatives and chose Jira.
03 / ORGANIZE
I structured a Kanban flow with owners, categories, priorities, statuses and deadlines.
04 / PRIORITIZE
I did not handle requests in arrival order. The main criteria were impact and user value.
05 / MONITOR
I used the IT Service Charter as the deadline reference and created an internal 24-hour buffer for monitoring.
06 / DECIDE
When the official deadline was missed, I had a basis to follow up with IT and, when applicable, request that the ticket be changed from request to incident.
07 / IMPROVE
I analyzed bottlenecks and looked for the causes of delays instead of simply demanding speed.
08 / SIMPLIFY
For access to a specific system, I agreed with IT to use screenshots to eliminate ambiguity when interpreting the request.
09 / VALIDATE
I did not close the ticket simply because IT responded. The user tested the solution and confirmed that the problem was resolved.
03 · WHAT CHANGED

When the flow became visible, the bottlenecks appeared

ACCESS

Less interpretation. Less rework.

Some requests took longer because the user had to describe every menu they needed access to. The screenshot made it clear what should be granted and reduced cases where the access provided differed from what was requested.

TECHNICAL VISITS

Exceptions became visible in the flow

Requests requiring a technical visit were also tracked. When the reference deadline was exceeded, I had evidence to challenge the handling and reclassify the request when necessary.

USER

The response was no longer the end

When IT responded, I went back to the user and asked them to test. Only after confirmation that it worked was the ticket considered complete.

MANAGERS

The result became visible

Managers began coming to me because they noticed that requests that previously took a week, and in some cases even a month, were being resolved faster.

04 · RESULT AND LEARNING

The number shows the result. The process explains why.

RESULT
71% Lead Time reduction, with requests being resolved in as little as 48 hours.

Monitoring combined Jira charts with conversations held during deliveries. The main learning was simple: before trying to speed up a process, it is worth understanding where the user loses control, where information arrives incomplete and where the flow starts to stall.
