CASE 02 · LEAD TIME GOVERNANCE · DOCUMENTATION

Case 02 Supporting Documents

English supporting documentation translated from the Portuguese source. It preserves the original scope and evidence boundaries.

01 · Executive summary

Discovery and diagnosis

The project addressed the need to organize technology requests submitted by employees of the Secretariat of Tax and Fiscal Management (SEGET), particularly in taxpayer service and tax operations. Requests involving access, infrastructure, equipment and other technology needs arrived through decentralized channels, without a structured curation layer between employees and the Department of Information and Communication Technology (TIC).

The Undersecretariat of Planning and Modernization created a portal to centralize requests. It took responsibility for intake guidance, curation, routing and follow-up, acting as the journey's focal point. Jira supported ticket management, deadline monitoring and escalation.

02 · Process design

AS IS and TO BE workflow

Before

Employees submitted requests individually and had to identify how and with whom to address each issue. TIC received requests directly, with limited curation and follow-up.

After

Employees registered requests through the portal. Planning and Modernization reviewed and classified them, managed tickets in Jira, routed work to TIC, monitored progress and supported user validation before closure.

  1. 01 · IntakeEmployee identifies a need and submits it through the portal.
  2. 02 · CurationPlanning and Modernization reviews context, documentation and request type.
  3. 03 · TrackingThe ticket is recorded and monitored in Jira.
  4. 04 · ExecutionTIC performs the technical work and provides an update.
  5. 05 · ValidationThe employee tests the solution; corrections are requested when needed.
  6. 06 · ClosureThe ticket is closed after the user confirms that the need has been met.
03 · Governance

Governance and prioritization framework

The governance cycle combined intake, prioritization, monitoring, escalation and continuous improvement. Decisions considered service criticality, the IT Service Charter deadline, operational impact, dependencies, access security and user validation.

Governance did not end when a request was routed to TIC. The focal point followed the request through resolution, identified deviations, contacted the responsible area, recorded learnings and fed improvements back into the process.

04 · Decision making

Decision and escalation playbook

  1. Is the request clear?If not, return it for curation and guide the employee on the form, documentation or ticket type.
  2. Is the classification correct?Check whether it is an incident or service request based on the Service Charter and context.
  3. Is there progress?If the ticket remains inactive beyond the internal monitoring point, contact TIC.
  4. Has the Service Charter deadline passed?Request an update and, when justified, review the request classification.
  5. Has the solution been validated?The employee tests the solution. Incorrect or incomplete outcomes return for correction.

Escalation triggers included missed deadlines, blocked work, incorrect delivery and inappropriate access permissions.

05 · Stakeholders

Stakeholder alignment

SEGET

Client area and beneficiary of improved service predictability.

Planning and Modernization

Responsible for process governance, request curation and follow-up.

TIC

Technical partner responsible for executing requests.

SEGET employees

Users who received guidance, progress updates and the opportunity to validate outcomes.

The main change was a clear communication layer. Employees no longer needed to negotiate directly with TIC, and TIC did not need to contact each employee individually.

06 · Success measures

Outcome and process health

The main quantitative outcome was an approximately 71% reduction in lead time, from about one week to up to 48 hours. No additional numerical results are claimed in this document.

Qualitative process-health dimensions included standardized intake, defined decision criteria, active governance, structured communication, user validation and access security. These dimensions describe observed process maturity, not additional measured percentages.

07 · Lessons learned

What the work reinforced

  • Technology helped structure the workflow, but the central challenge was changing behaviors and building trust in a centralized journey.
  • Flow problems need clear ownership. Active monitoring makes it possible to act before requests become stalled.
  • A technical response is not the same as a resolution. User validation confirms whether the need was actually met.
  • Curation improves request quality and reduces ambiguity and rework.
  • Access governance is also a security measure: permissions should reflect the employee's actual duties rather than simply copying another profile.

The central learning was that efficient processes begin with understanding people's needs and building governance that supports decisions over time. The result came from combining people, process, technology and governance, not from a tool alone.
