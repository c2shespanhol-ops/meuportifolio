CASE 06 · PEIXE URBANO · CUSTOMER EXPERIENCE · SERVICE DESIGN

Less friction.
More time to enjoy.

How proactive outreach and assisted booking contributed to a 37% reduction in the volume of cancellations and purchase withdrawals among Premium-segment customers purchasing tourism and accommodation coupons.

Context
Peixe Urbano
November 2015 to March 2017
Segment
Premium segment
Exclusive to buyers of tourism and accommodation coupons
Tool
Zendesk
Operational case tracking
Role
Initiative and execution
Opportunity identified and operational flow carried out directly
01 · CONTEXT AND PROBLEM

Buying was simple. Redeeming was not always simple.

Customers purchased coupons for experiences, often including accommodation. To redeem the benefit, they still had to contact the establishment, check availability, negotiate dates and arrange the booking. This extra work could turn purchase excitement into operational effort and contribute to withdrawal.

The issue was not necessarily the purchased benefit itself. It also involved the work customers had to do after purchase to actually use it.
02 · DISCOVERY AND HYPOTHESIS

Reducing effort could help more customers complete the experience.

The opportunity came from observing the operation and drawing on previous call-center experience. Rather than waiting for customers to handle every booking step on their own, the proposal was to reach out proactively and offer practical assistance.

Observed opportunity

Premium Tourism customers had to coordinate availability and bookings directly with partners after purchasing the benefit.

Working hypothesis

If the operation took over booking coordination, perceived effort would decrease, barriers would be removed and coupon redemption could increase before the withdrawal deadline.

This hypothesis is a retrospective reconstruction of the reasoning behind the initiative. It does not represent a controlled experiment or formal hypothesis validation.

03 · SOLUTION AND EXECUTION

Proactive outreach and assisted booking, from purchase to redemption.

01 · IDENTIFICATION

Target segment

A daily mailing list was received with customers who had purchased coupons the previous day, within the Premium Tourism segment.
02 · OUTREACH

Offer of assistance

The team sent a proactive email introducing the assisted-booking service and explaining that it could handle the contact with the establishment.
03 · OPT-IN

Information collection

Interested customers replied with the requested information. Assistance depended on the customer expressing interest.
04 · PARTNER COORDINATION

Availability and booking

The team contacted the partner, checked availability, negotiated alternative dates and times when needed, and sought booking confirmation.
05 · FOLLOW-UP

Confirmation through redemption

Follow-up covered the journey from purchase and booking confirmation to checking whether the customer had successfully redeemed the coupon.
06 · POST-EXPERIENCE

Feedback and service recovery

An NPS survey was sent after the experience. Reported issues were addressed to minimize customer impact. In severe cases, a refund could be offered subject to management approval.

Zendesk was used to record and track cases. The process became part of the team's regular routine.

04 · SERVICE JOURNEY

A journey that transfers less work to the customer.

Without assistance

Purchase coupon → customer contacts partner → checks availability → negotiates dates → confirms booking → redeems benefit.

With assistance

Purchase coupon → proactive outreach → customer chooses to reply → team coordinates booking → confirmation → redemption follow-up.

Retrospective journey representation. It is not a formal user-research record or an experimental comparison of task times.

05 · OUTCOMES AND METRICS

Fewer withdrawals. More coupons redeemed within 7 days.

37%Relative reduction in the volume of cancellations and purchase withdrawals, comparing the periods before and after the initiative.
↑Reported increase in coupon redemption within 7 days. No absolute volume or growth percentage is available.
↑Reported increase in customer satisfaction: customers knew they would be contacted after purchase and offered booking assistance. No numerical NPS change is documented.

The 37% figure was reported as a reduction in volume in a before-and-after comparison. Absolute volumes, exact comparison windows and base composition are not available in this record. It should not be read as a 37-percentage-point decrease or as standalone proof of causality. The supporting documentation includes charts with illustrative retrospective estimates, clearly separated from the reported historical outcomes.

06 · GOVERNANCE AND COLLABORATION

An operational routine connecting customers, partners and management.

Customer operations

Customer outreach, information collection, booking coordination, follow-up and issue handling.

Partners

Availability checks, date and time alignment, and booking confirmation.

Management

Approval for refunds in severe cases and periodic review of performance indicators.

Monitoring

Periodic reports and meetings covering cancellations, coupon redemption and NPS, with suggestions for process adjustments.

07 · LEARNING

Experience is not only completing a task. It is removing unnecessary effort.

Call-center experience helped identify that anticipating a need can be more effective than waiting for a complaint. At Peixe Urbano, this learning was applied to a different journey: instead of only responding to customers, the team offered convenience and took on part of the coordination required to make a booking.

Perceived value was not only the price of the benefit, but also the time customers no longer had to spend handling administrative steps.
08 · SCOPE AND LIMITATIONS

What this case demonstrates, and what it does not claim.

  • The initiative was proposed based on an operational opportunity and executed directly by the professional.
  • The service targeted Premium Tourism, not the entire customer base.
  • Assistance was offered; customers had to reply and express interest.
  • Zendesk supported case recording and tracking.
  • Refunds required management approval.
  • No evidence is available of A/B testing, formal interviews, documented personas or a numerical increase in NPS.
  • The journey map and blueprint associated with this case are retrospective analytical artifacts, not documents proven to have been used at the time.
