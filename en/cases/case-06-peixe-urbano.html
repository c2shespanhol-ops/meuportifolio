Case 06 · Urban Fish · Customer Experience and Service Design

Less friction, more time to enjoy

How assisted booking and proactive outreach helped reduce cancellations and purchase withdrawals among Premium customers buying tourism and accommodation coupons.

About 3 min read

Reported result
37% fewer cancellations and purchase withdrawals, comparing the periods before and after the initiative
Context
Urban Fish · November 2015 to March 2017
Segment
Premium, exclusively for tourism and accommodation coupons
Tool
Zendesk, for case recording and tracking
Contribution
Initiative identified and carried out by me within Operations

My role: I identified the operational opportunity and carried out assisted booking, from proactive outreach to follow-up and the post-experience survey. Evidence boundary: the 37% figure was reported as a reduction in volume before and after the initiative. Absolute volumes, exact time windows and cohort composition are unavailable, so the result is not a 37-percentage-point decrease or standalone proof of causality. There is no evidence of A/B testing, formal interviews, documented personas or a numerical increase in NPS.

01 · Context and problem

Buying was simple. Redeeming the benefit was not always.

Effort after purchase

To redeem the coupon, customers still had to contact the establishment, check availability, negotiate dates and arrange the booking.

Where the friction occurred

The problem was not necessarily the purchased benefit itself, but the work customers had to do afterward to use it.

02 · Hypothesis

If we reduced the effort, more customers could complete the experience

Opportunity

Premium customers had to coordinate availability and bookings with partners after making a purchase.

Working hypothesis

By taking over booking coordination, Operations could reduce perceived effort and encourage coupon redemption before the withdrawal deadline.

This hypothesis is an analytical reconstruction of the reasoning at the time. It was not a controlled experiment or formal validation.

03 · Execution

Proactive outreach and assisted booking

  1. Audience selectionA daily mailing list identified Premium Tourism customers who had purchased the previous day.
  2. Offer of assistanceThe team sent a proactive email offering assisted booking.
  3. AdesãoInterested customers replied with the requested information. Assistance depended on that response.
  4. Contato com o parceiroThe team checked availability, negotiated dates and times, and sought to confirm the booking.
  5. Follow-upFollow-up ran from the request to confirmation and checking whether the customer had successfully redeemed the coupon.
  6. After the experienceAn NPS survey and issue resolution followed the experience. In severe cases, refunds required management approval.
04 · Results

Fewer withdrawals, with comparison basis and limitations

Volume before the initiative
100%
Volume after (relative reduction)
63%

Comparison basis: the chart uses 100 as an index for the previous volume to illustrate the 37% relative reduction. It does not represent actual volumes, which are unavailable. An increase in coupon redemption within 7 days and in satisfaction was also reported, but neither has a documented percentage or numerical change.

05 · Governance

A routine connecting customers, partners and management

Customer service

Customer outreach, information collection, booking coordination and issue handling.

Partners

Availability checks, date coordination and booking confirmation.

Management

Refund approval in severe cases and periodic review of performance indicators.

Monitoring

Periodic reports and meetings covering cancellations, coupon redemption and NPS.

06 · Learning

Experience means reducing unnecessary effort

Anticipate

My call-center experience showed that anticipating a need can work better than waiting for a complaint.

Perceived value

The value was not only the price of the benefit, but also the time customers no longer had to spend on administrative steps.
