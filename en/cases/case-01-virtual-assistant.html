CASE STUDY 01 · TAX & FISCAL MANAGEMENT

Building an Institutional Virtual Assistant

Product Discovery applied to the design of an institutional virtual assistant initially intended to support employees of the Tax and Fiscal Management Secretariat.

PRODUCT APPROACHDiscovery before technology
UsersSecretariat employees
ContributionProduct Discovery | UX
ContextInternal institutional support
FocusKnowledge, journey and conversational experience
01 · THE PRODUCT

A product thought through before implementation.

Primary user

Secretariat employees needing institutional communications, quick answers and internal procedures.

Product

An institutional virtual assistant designed to centralize knowledge and reduce operational dependency.

02 · DISCOVERY ARTIFACTS

From the problem to the experience architecture.

Product Vision

Objectives, scope, constraints and the intended vision for the institutional assistant.

Personas & User Journey

User profiles and interaction flows used to understand the people and context behind the service.

Conversational Architecture

Organization of intents and conversation structures for the assistant.

Requirements & Prototype

Functional requirements, tone of voice, master prompt and a conceptual prototype.

ROLE & OWNERSHIP

What I did and what this case proves.

My contribution: I structured Discovery, product vision, personas, journey, conversational architecture, tone of voice, functional requirements and the conceptual prototype. Limit: this case is not presented as evidence of formal ownership of Product Strategy, Product Backlog or product metrics.

03 · PRODUCT READING

From need to experience design.

Discovery

The work structured the need, users, journey and usage context before implementation.

Experience architecture

Conversational architecture, tone of voice, requirements and prototype translated the intended experience into tangible artifacts.

Validation

The persona was approved by management and adopted in internal Secretariat channels, including management presentations and meetings.

Evidence boundary

The case demonstrates Discovery, user understanding, journey mapping, requirements and institutional solution design without claiming formal product strategy ownership.
